Visiting Judges Case Manager — Support

Answers to the questions that come up, roughly in the order they come up. For a walk-through from setup to statements, see Getting started. Anything not covered here: jwsimoneaux@gmail.com.

Getting started

What does the app do?

It keeps every assignment you sit on in one place: the order of assignment, the documents, court settings and trips, deadlines with reminders, a pending list, notes, your time, and your expenses. From those it prepares the statement of time and expenses for each case, addressed to the judge who assigned you, and tracks it from submitted to paid. It is built for Texas judges sitting by assignment and runs on iPhone and iPad.

What should I set up first?

The first screens ask who you are, how you are paid, where your mileage starts, and who usually assigns you. Then add your first assignment: the cause, the court, the county seat, and the order of assignment, including the kind of assignment. That one choice sets how your time is counted and which statutes your statements cite. You can start with an invented sample case to look around, and remove it in Settings when you’re ready.

Why must I use Face ID or a passcode?

Because the app holds court records. It locks every time it opens and again after a minute in the background. The check is done by iOS; the app never sees your face data or passcode.

Assignments and pay

Which kinds of assignment does it handle?

Five, each with its own statute: a regional assignment by one of the eleven presiding judges of the administrative judicial regions (Tex. Gov’t Code § 74.056); a statutory probate court assignment by the presiding judge of the statutory probate courts (§ 25.0022(h)); a county court probate, guardianship, or mental health matter (§§ 26.012, 25.0022(h)); a visiting judge appointed by the county judge (§§ 26.023, 26.026); and a temporary justice of the peace (§ 27.055(b)). For anything else, choose Other and type the authority your order cites.

Why is my time in days on one case and hours on another?

Because the statutes differ. Under Chapter 74, sitting more than half a day is a full day and half a day or less is a half day (§ 74.061(j)); the app counts each date once. Section 25.0022 has no half-day rule, and the statutory probate courts’ report form asks for actual hours, in person and remote, so a statutory probate case is always in hours: a 15-minute review of a motion is 0.25 hour, not a half day.

What does a statutory probate statement look like?

It follows the Assigned Statutory Probate Judge’s Report: the assignment, total hours worked in person and remotely, the days with in-person service, the expense lines, and the form’s certification, followed by a sheet of hours and dates and the itemized expenses with receipts. It claims no dollar amount for time, because the presiding judge determines the salary.

How is mileage figured?

Round trip from the origin you set, at the rate in effect on the day you leave: $0.725 a mile from January 1, 2026, the rate the Office of Court Administration uses. Past years’ rates are built in, and you can change a rate in Settings if your assigning judge uses another. Lodging, meals, and other expenses are claimed at actual cost, with receipts attached to the statement.

What about the $25 per diem, or a statutory county court?

Log the per diem as its own expense, or let a trip do it: once you set your per diem in Settings, Log trip expenses on a trip claims the per diem for every day away, the day you leave and the day you return included. Chapter 74 pays it for each day or fraction of a day outside your district or county (§ 74.061(f)); § 25.0022(p) provides the same for statutory probate assignments. For an assignment to a statutory county court, turn off Time is compensated for this assignment on the case: the State pays no compensation for that sitting (§ 74.061(k)), so your time is listed for the record while your expenses are still claimed.

Where do the explanations and citations come from?

Tap any ⓘ for the rule in plain words and the statute behind it. They summarize the Government Code, Estates Code, and Property Code as current on January 1, 2026, the Code of Criminal Procedure as amended through the 2025 session, the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure as amended through July 1, 2026, the Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure as amended through February 6, 2026, and the Office of Court Administration’s travel guidance. They are not legal advice, and your assigning judge’s own procedures govern the paperwork.

Court settings and trips

What is a court setting?

Anything set on the court’s calendar for one of your cases: a hearing, a trial, a pretrial or status conference, a mediation, or something else you name. A setting can run several days. By default it counts court days only, skipping weekends and national holidays; turn that off for a setting that sits straight through. Each setting is in person or remote. To change or delete a setting or a trip, tap Edit on it.

Why are trips separate from settings?

Because travel doesn’t line up with the docket. A trip runs from the day you leave to the day you come home and may serve one setting, a week of them, or none at all (a day at the courthouse to review the file, say). When you add an in-person setting, the app offers Plan travel with dates suggested by the distance: leave the day before when the courthouse is 150 miles or more away, and come home the day after when it is 250 or more. Change them as you like.

How do I claim a trip?

Once the trip has begun, it offers Log trip expenses: one round trip of mileage at the rate on the day you left, and the per diem for each day away. Then it opens an expense for the hotel bill, so you can attach the receipt.

What goes on my calendar?

Choose a calendar in Settings first. Then Add to calendar on a setting adds a timed event for each day you sit, and on a trip an all-day event from the day you leave to the day you return. Change or delete the setting or trip in the app and the calendar follows, on that device. Open deadlines are copied to the calendar automatically (see below).

What happened to my hearings and travel days?

They carried over. Each hearing is now a court setting, and a hearing’s travel day and lodging became a trip of its own, which keeps the calendar event you had already added.

Deadlines and reminders

How do I add deadlines?

Set the type of case on the case (civil, criminal, decedent’s estate, guardianship, or trust), then tap Start deadlines. Pick what happened, such as letters granted, a judgment signed, or a request for findings filed, and its date. The app proposes every deadline that runs from it, with its citation; uncheck any that don’t apply. You can also add a deadline of your own for anything the rules don’t cover.

Which deadlines are built in?

Estates: qualifying, notice to will beneficiaries and the affidavit of notice, the inventory, and the annual account in a dependent administration. Guardianships: qualifying, the inventory, the monthly allowance, the investment plan, the annual account, and the annual report on the ward. Civil motions, in any case outside the criminal rules: a Rule 91a motion to dismiss (the motion, the response, the last day to nonsuit or amend, and your ruling within 45 days), and summary judgment under Rule 166a as amended for motions filed on or after March 1, 2026 (the response 21 days after filing, the reply, setting the hearing or submission by day 60, proposed orders, and your written ruling within 90 days after it is heard). What the court owes, outside criminal cases: findings of fact and conclusions of law, the ruling on a motion for new trial, the end of plenary power, and the order on a motion to recuse. Trusts: the trustee’s statement of accounts on a beneficiary’s written demand; notice to the Attorney General in a proceeding involving a charitable trust or gift, both 30 days after filing and 25 days before a hearing; and a management trust’s initial report and annual accounts. Criminal: after sentence, the certification of the right of appeal, the motion for new trial (and the 75 days to rule on it), presentment, and the notices of appeal; the State’s notice of appeal; release when the State is not ready for trial; the State’s response to a motion for DNA testing; the 180 days for shock community supervision; and the habeas timetables under articles 11.07 and 11.072. The recusal deadline is offered in criminal cases too. When the fiduciary qualifies, mark that deadline met and the app offers the ones that run from qualification; mark a year’s account met and the next year’s is added.

How are the days counted?

As Tex. Gov’t Code § 311.014 and Tex. R. Civ. P. 4 count them: the day of the event is not counted and the last day is; a period that ends on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday runs to the next day that is none of those; and a period of five days or less skips them throughout (criminal periods count the same way under Tex. R. App. P. 4.1, without the five-day rule). Only the national holidays on which every courthouse closes are built in; for a county’s other closures, change the date yourself. A deadline you owe is never moved later: acting by the earlier date is always safe. For the same reason, a period counted back from a hearing (notice at least 25 days before) moves earlier when it lands on a weekend or holiday.

A motion to extend was filed. What do I do?

Open the deadline and tap Motion to extend filed. The deadline stands, and a to-do to rule on the motion is added. Grant it with the new date and the signed order, or deny it; to extend on the court’s own motion, use Extend by order. Every extension is kept with the deadline, and the last one can be undone. The new-trial periods can’t be extended (Tex. R. Civ. P. 5), so the app doesn’t offer it.

When do reminders come, and what do they say?

At 8:00 a.m., 30 days, 7 days, and 1 day before a deadline and on the day itself; change the days on any deadline. Allow notifications when the app asks. To keep case names off the lock screen, a reminder says only that a deadline is due unless you turn on Show case names in reminders in Settings. The app’s icon shows how many deadlines are overdue. Reminders are scheduled on the device itself; nothing is sent to a server.

Do deadlines go on my calendar?

Yes, once you choose a calendar in Settings. Each open deadline appears as an all-day event, updated when it changes and removed when it is met or waived. Turn off Copy deadlines to my calendar in Settings if you would rather not.

Bonds

Which bonds can I keep?

Every kind, in every case: a judge sitting in a probate matter hears TROs, receiverships, and cost contests too. The Bonds tab on a case lists the kinds that fit the case type first, then all the rest: executors, administrators, temporary administrators and guardians, guardians, management trustees and other trustees; TRO and temporary-injunction bonds; supersedeas; the receivership applicant’s bond and the receiver’s own bond; attachment, garnishment, sequestration, distress-warrant and replevy bonds; security for costs and statements of inability; appeals from justice court (civil, eviction, and criminal); and bail. Tap ⓘ beside a kind for its rule.

What does a bond record keep?

Who gives it and to whom it is payable, the amount, the form (corporate surety, personal sureties, cash, deposit, personal bond, statement of inability, or waived, with the reason), the sureties, who approves it, the dates it was ordered, due, filed, approved, and released, and a history of every change by order (set, increased, reduced, new bond, sureties changed, released or forfeited) with the signed order attached. A bail bond also keeps who set it and its conditions, and records surrender, revocation, and forfeiture.

How does the app compare a fiduciary’s bond with the estate?

For an executor, administrator, or guardian of the estate, the Estates Code sets the bond at the value of the personal property plus the revenue expected in the next 12 months, less assets on deposit by court order (§§ 305.153, 1105.154); a management trustee’s bond is the principal plus a year’s income (§ 1301.058). Enter the figures from the inventory, each annual account, or a sale, and the app shows the formula amount beside the bond. It flags a bond below the formula as possibly insufficient, and one above it by more than the margin you set in Settings as possibly reducible. It decides nothing: whether a bond protects the estate is your judgment.

And on appeal?

For supersedeas on a money judgment, enter the compensatory damages, interest for the appeal, costs, and (if shown) the debtor’s net worth, and the app figures the security: the sum, capped at the lesser of half the net worth or $25 million (Tex. R. App. P. 24.2; Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 52.006).

Which bond deadlines are built in?

A temporary administrator’s bond within 3 business days (state holidays skipped too); a bond ordered from an exempt executor or guardian within 10 days; your yearly review of each guardian’s bond (§ 1201.002), offered automatically when the guardian qualifies; a TRO’s 14 days; your ruling on a motion to dissolve a writ within 10 days; notice before appointing a receiver; findings within 7 days of appointing a receiver in a divorce; a rule for costs; the justice-court appeal deadlines and the county court’s hearing and trial deadlines; the § 11a bail timetable, a review of bail on the State’s request, and the surety’s answer in a forfeiture. Start them from Bond deadlines on the Bonds tab.

Statements

Who receives my statement?

The judge who assigned the case, chosen on each case: any of the eleven regional presiding judges, the presiding judge of the statutory probate courts, or someone you type in, such as a county judge. Names and addresses come from the Office of Court Administration’s published list and can be corrected on the case if an office changes.

How do I send one, and how do I keep track?

Open Prepare statement on the case. Review it, then print or save the PDF, or email it with the receipts attached. Mark it submitted when you send it: its entries lock, so nothing is ever claimed twice, and the next statement starts fresh. Mark it forwarded for payment and paid as it moves along; Time & Statements shows where every statement stands.

Your records

Where is my data? Is there an account?

No account and no server of ours. Everything lives on your device and nothing reaches us. See the privacy policy, which is short because there is little to disclose.

How do I back up, or move to a new iPad?

In Settings, Back up with documents writes everything to one file that you save where you like: Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or email. On the new device, choose Restore from a backup when the app first opens, or Restore in Settings later. Restoring merges with what is already there, so you can also use a backup to bring an iPhone and an iPad into agreement. A device backup or Apple’s Quick Start also carries the app’s data to a new device.

Can I use larger text?

Yes. The app follows the text size set in Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size. At larger sizes, the iPad switches to the single-column layout so nothing is squeezed.

Subscription

What does it cost?

The download is free. A subscription is $14.99 for the first year, then $49.99 a year. It renews automatically unless you turn it off at least 24 hours before the renewal date. Payment is charged to your Apple Account.

Why a subscription?

Because the rules change. The Legislature amends the statutes, the mileage rate changes each January, and presiding judges and their offices change. The subscription pays for keeping the app’s rules, rates, directory, and explanations current.

How do I cancel, or restore a purchase on a new device?

Cancel any time in Settings → your name → Subscriptions on your iPhone or iPad. To use your subscription on another device signed into the same Apple Account, tap Restore purchases in the app’s Settings.

What happens if my subscription ends?

Your records stay yours. The app becomes read-only: you can open every case, print past statements, and back up or export everything, but you can’t add or change anything until you renew.
