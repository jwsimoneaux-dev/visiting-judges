Visiting Judges Case Manager — Support

Answers to the questions that come up, roughly in the order they come up. For a walk-through from setup to statements, see Getting started. Anything not covered here: jwsimoneaux@gmail.com.

Getting started

What does the app do?

It keeps every assignment you sit on in one place: the order of assignment, the documents, court settings and trips, deadlines with reminders, a pending list, notes, your time, and your expenses. From those it prepares the statement of time and expenses for each case, addressed to the judge who assigned you, and tracks it from submitted to paid. It is built for Texas judges sitting by assignment and runs on Windows 10 and 11.

How do I install it?

Download the installer (the .exe file) and run it. It installs for your own Windows account, with no administrator rights needed. Windows may say it protected your PC from an unrecognized app: choose More info, then Run anyway. If your county manages your PC and the installer is blocked, ask its IT staff to allow it.

What should I set up first?

The first screens ask who you are, how you are paid, where your mileage starts, and who usually assigns you. Then add your first assignment: the cause, the court, the county seat, and the order of assignment, including the kind of assignment. That one choice sets how your time is counted and which statutes your statements cite. You can start with an invented sample case to look around, and remove it in Settings when you’re ready.

Why must I unlock it every time?

Because it holds court records. It locks each time it opens, after a minute away, and whenever Windows locks or sleeps. It uses Windows Hello (face, fingerprint, or your Windows PIN) when your PC has it set up; otherwise it asks for a PIN of its own, 6 to 12 digits, which you choose the first time it opens.

I forgot the program’s PIN. What now?

The PIN cannot be recovered, because the program keeps only a scrambled form of it, but you can choose a new one without losing anything. On the screen that asks for the PIN, click Forgot PIN? and send the request number it shows to jwsimoneaux@gmail.com. After confirming it is you, we email a reset code to the address your code was issued to: paste it, and choose a new PIN. The code works once, only on that PC, for one day. You may close the program while you wait.

No Forgot PIN? on that screen means the program is older than version 1.1.5. Quit the program (right-click its icon in the tray by the clock and choose Quit). Press Windows key + R, enter %LOCALAPPDATA%\Visiting Judges Case Manager\Records, and delete the file lock.json (and lock.json.bak, if it is there). Start the program again and choose a new PIN; your records are untouched.

Where did the program go when I closed it?

To the system tray, by the clock, so that deadline reminders keep arriving. Click its icon to bring it back. To close it completely, right-click the icon and choose Quit.

Assignments and pay

Which kinds of assignment does it handle?

Five, each with its own statute: a regional assignment by one of the eleven presiding judges of the administrative judicial regions (Tex. Gov’t Code § 74.056); a statutory probate court assignment by the presiding judge of the statutory probate courts (§ 25.0022(h)); a county court probate, guardianship, or mental health matter (§§ 26.012, 25.0022(h)); a visiting judge appointed by the county judge (§§ 26.023, 26.026); and a temporary justice of the peace (§ 27.055(b)). For anything else, choose Other and type the authority your order cites.

Why is my time in days on one case and hours on another?

Because the statutes differ. Under Chapter 74, sitting more than half a day is a full day and half a day or less is a half day (§ 74.061(j)); the app counts each date once. Section 25.0022 has no half-day rule, and the statutory probate courts’ report form asks for actual hours, in person and remote, so a statutory probate case is always in hours: a 15-minute review of a motion is 0.25 hour, not a half day.

What does a statutory probate statement look like?

It follows the Assigned Statutory Probate Judge’s Report: the assignment, total hours worked in person and remotely, the days with in-person service, the expense lines, and the form’s certification, followed by a sheet of hours and dates and the itemized expenses with receipts. It claims no dollar amount for time, because the presiding judge determines the salary.

How is mileage figured?

Round trip from the origin you set, at the rate in effect on the day you leave: $0.725 a mile from January 1, 2026, and $0.76 from July 1, 2026, when the IRS raised it mid-year; the Texas Comptroller adopts the same rates for state travel. A newly published rate is added to your table flagged “verify” until you confirm it. Past years’ rates are built in, and you can change a rate in Settings if your assigning judge uses another. Lodging, meals, and other expenses are claimed at actual cost, with receipts attached to the statement.

What about the $25 per diem, or a statutory county court?

Log the per diem as its own expense, or let a trip do it: once you set your per diem in Settings, Log trip expenses on a trip claims the per diem for every day away, the day you leave and the day you return included. Chapter 74 pays it for each day or fraction of a day outside your district or county (§ 74.061(f)); § 25.0022(p) provides the same for statutory probate assignments, paid by the county in which you served. No other kind of assignment carries one. For an assignment to a statutory county court, turn off Time is compensated for this assignment on the case: the State pays no compensation for that sitting (§ 74.061(k)), so your time is listed for the record while your expenses are still claimed.

Where do the explanations and citations come from?

Click any ⓘ for the rule in plain words and the statute behind it. They summarize the Government Code, Estates Code, and Property Code as current on January 1, 2026, the Code of Criminal Procedure as amended through the 2025 session, the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure as amended through October 1, 2026, the Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure as amended through February 6, 2026, and the Office of Court Administration’s travel guidance. They are not legal advice, and your assigning judge’s own procedures govern the paperwork.

Court settings and trips

What is a court setting?

Anything set on the court’s calendar for one of your cases: a hearing, a trial, a pretrial or status conference, a mediation, or something else you name. A setting can run several days. By default it counts court days only, skipping weekends and national holidays; turn that off for a setting that sits straight through. Each setting is in person or remote. To change or delete a setting or a trip, click Edit on it.

Why are trips separate from settings?

Because travel doesn’t line up with the docket. A trip runs from the day you leave to the day you come home and may serve one setting, a week of them, or none at all (a day at the courthouse to review the file, say). When you add an in-person setting, the app offers Plan travel with dates suggested by the distance: leave the day before when the courthouse is 150 miles or more away, and come home the day after when it is 250 or more. Change them as you like.

How do I claim a trip?

Once the trip has begun, it offers Log trip expenses: one round trip of mileage at the rate on the day you left, and the per diem for each day away. Then it opens an expense for the hotel bill, so you can attach the receipt.

What goes on my calendar?

Whatever you put there. Settings → Calendar → Save calendar file (.ics) saves your upcoming court settings (an event for each day you sit), your trips (from the day you leave to the day you return), and your open deadlines. Open the file in Outlook, or import it into Google Calendar. When things change, save a new file and open it again: the same events are updated instead of copied.

Deadlines and reminders

How do I add deadlines?

Set the type of case on the case (civil, criminal, decedent’s estate, guardianship, or trust), then click Start deadlines. Pick what happened, such as letters granted, a judgment signed, or a request for findings filed, and its date. The app proposes every deadline that runs from it, with its citation; uncheck any that don’t apply. You can also add a deadline of your own for anything the rules don’t cover.

Which deadlines are built in?

Estates: qualifying, notice to will beneficiaries and the affidavit of notice, the inventory, and the annual account in a dependent administration. Guardianships: qualifying, the inventory, the monthly allowance, the investment plan, the annual account, and the annual report on the ward. Civil motions, in any case outside the criminal rules: a Rule 91a motion to dismiss (the motion, the response, the last day to nonsuit or amend, and your ruling within 45 days), and summary judgment under Rule 166a as amended for motions filed on or after March 1, 2026 (the response 21 days after filing, the reply, setting the hearing or submission by day 60, proposed orders, and your written ruling within 90 days after it is heard). What the court owes, outside criminal cases: findings of fact and conclusions of law, the ruling on a motion for new trial, the end of plenary power, and the order on a motion to recuse. Trusts: the trustee’s statement of accounts on a beneficiary’s written demand; notice to the Attorney General in a proceeding involving a charitable trust or gift, both 30 days after filing and 25 days before a hearing; and a management trust’s initial report and annual accounts. Criminal: after sentence, the certification of the right of appeal, the motion for new trial (and the 75 days to rule on it), presentment, and the notices of appeal; the State’s notice of appeal; release when the State is not ready for trial; the State’s response to a motion for DNA testing; the 180 days for shock community supervision; and the habeas timetables under articles 11.07 and 11.072. The recusal deadline is offered in criminal cases too. When the fiduciary qualifies, mark that deadline met and the app offers the ones that run from qualification; mark a year’s account met and the next year’s is added.

How are the days counted?

As Tex. Gov’t Code § 311.014 and Tex. R. Civ. P. 4 count them: the day of the event is not counted and the last day is; a period that ends on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday runs to the next day that is none of those; and a period of five days or less skips them throughout (criminal periods count the same way under Tex. R. App. P. 4.1, without the five-day rule). Only the national holidays on which every courthouse closes are built in; for a county’s other closures, change the date yourself. A deadline you owe is never moved later: acting by the earlier date is always safe. For the same reason, a period counted back from a hearing (notice at least 25 days before) moves earlier when it lands on a weekend or holiday.

A motion to extend was filed. What do I do?

Open the deadline and click Motion to extend filed. The deadline stands, and a to-do to rule on the motion is added. Grant it with the new date and the signed order, or deny it; to extend on the court’s own motion, use Extend by order. Every extension is kept with the deadline, and the last one can be undone. The new-trial periods can’t be extended (Tex. R. Civ. P. 5), so the app doesn’t offer it.

When do reminders come, and what do they say?

As Windows notifications at 8:00 a.m., 30 days, 7 days, and 1 day before a deadline and on the day itself; change the days on any deadline. They come while the program is open or in the tray, so close the window rather than quitting. A reminder says only that a deadline is due unless you turn on Show case names in reminders in Settings. Hold the pointer over the tray icon to see how many deadlines are overdue. Reminders come from the program on your PC; nothing is sent to a server.

Do deadlines go on my calendar?

Yes, through the calendar file in Settings: each open deadline is an all-day event in it. Save a new file after deadlines change, and open it again to bring your calendar up to date.

Bonds

Which bonds can I keep?

Every kind, in every case: a judge sitting in a probate matter hears TROs, receiverships, and cost contests too. The Bonds tab on a case lists the kinds that fit the case type first, then all the rest: executors, administrators, temporary administrators and guardians, guardians, management trustees and other trustees; TRO and temporary-injunction bonds; supersedeas; the receivership applicant’s bond and the receiver’s own bond; attachment, garnishment, sequestration, distress-warrant and replevy bonds; security for costs and statements of inability; appeals from justice court (civil, eviction, and criminal); and bail. Click ⓘ beside a kind for its rule.

What does a bond record keep?

Who gives it and to whom it is payable, the amount, the form (corporate surety, personal sureties, cash, deposit, personal bond, statement of inability, or waived, with the reason), the sureties, who approves it, the dates it was ordered, due, filed, approved, and released, and a history of every change by order (set, increased, reduced, new bond, sureties changed, released or forfeited) with the signed order attached. A bail bond also keeps who set it and its conditions, and records surrender, revocation, and forfeiture.

How does the app compare a fiduciary’s bond with the estate?

For an executor, administrator, or guardian of the estate, the Estates Code sets the bond at the value of the personal property plus the revenue expected in the next 12 months, less assets on deposit by court order (§§ 305.153, 1105.154); a management trustee’s bond is the principal plus a year’s income (§ 1301.058). Enter the figures from the inventory, each annual account, or a sale, and the app shows the formula amount beside the bond. It flags a bond below the formula as possibly insufficient, and one above it by more than the margin you set in Settings as possibly reducible. It decides nothing: whether a bond protects the estate is your judgment.

And on appeal?

For supersedeas on a money judgment, enter the compensatory damages, interest for the appeal, costs, and (if shown) the debtor’s net worth, and the app figures the security: the sum, capped at the lesser of half the net worth or $25 million (Tex. R. App. P. 24.2; Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 52.006).

Which bond deadlines are built in?

A temporary administrator’s bond within 3 business days (state holidays skipped too); a bond ordered from an exempt executor or guardian within 10 days; your yearly review of each guardian’s bond (§ 1201.002), offered automatically when the guardian qualifies; a TRO’s 14 days; your ruling on a motion to dissolve a writ within 10 days; notice before appointing a receiver; findings within 7 days of appointing a receiver in a divorce; a rule for costs; the justice-court appeal deadlines and the county court’s hearing and trial deadlines; the § 11a bail timetable, a review of bail on the State’s request, and the surety’s answer in a forfeiture. Start them from Bond deadlines on the Bonds tab.

Statements

Who receives my statement?

The judge who assigned the case, chosen on each case: any of the eleven regional presiding judges, the presiding judge of the statutory probate courts, or someone you type in, such as a county judge. Names and addresses come from the Office of Court Administration’s published list and can be corrected on the case if an office changes.

How do I send one, and how do I keep track?

Open Prepare statement on the case. Review it, then print or save the PDF. Email saves the statement and its receipts to a folder, opens the folder, and starts a draft to the assigning judge in your email program; attach the files and send it yourself. Mark it submitted when you send it: its entries lock, so nothing is ever claimed twice, and the next statement starts fresh. Mark it forwarded for payment and paid as it moves along; Time & Statements shows where every statement stands.

Your records

Where is my data? Is there an account?

No account and no server of ours. Everything lives in your own Windows account on your PC (Settings → Privacy → Show where my records are kept opens the folder), and nothing reaches us. See the privacy policy, which is short because there is little to disclose.

How do I back up, or move to a new PC?

In Settings, Back up with documents writes everything to one .zip file that you save where you like: a folder, a USB drive, or OneDrive. On the new PC, install the program, choose Restore from a backup when it first opens (or Restore in Settings later), and enter your code again. Restoring merges with what is already there, so you can also use a backup to bring two PCs into agreement.

Can I use larger text?

Yes. The program follows Windows’ display scale (Settings → System → Display → Scale). At larger sizes, or in a narrow window, it switches to a single-column layout so nothing is squeezed.

Your code and updates

What does it cost, and why do I need a code?

Nothing. The program is free, but adding and changing records takes a personal code from the publisher, good for a year at a time. To ask for one, write to jwsimoneaux@gmail.com. Enter it in Settings → Windows code → Enter a code; Settings then shows whom it is issued to and the date it is good through.

What happens when my code runs out?

Your records stay yours. The program becomes read-only: you can open every case, print past statements, and back up or export everything, but you can’t add or change anything until you enter a new code under Enter a new code.

How do updates work?

Statutes are amended, the mileage rate changes, and presiding judges and their offices change, so new versions come out. About a minute after the program starts, and every six hours after that, it checks for a newer version and downloads it; the update installs the next time the program restarts. Settings → Updates shows your version, checks at once, offers Restart and install when an update is ready, and can turn automatic checks off. Your records, PIN, and code carry over.

Still stuck, or found a rule or a figure that doesn’t look right? Write to jwsimoneaux@gmail.com with what you saw and what you expected. Reports of a changed statute or a changed office address are especially welcome.
