Visiting Judges Case Manager — Support

Answers to the questions that come up, roughly in the order they come up. Anything not covered here: jwsimoneaux@gmail.com.

Getting started

What does the app do?

It keeps every assignment you sit on in one place: the order of assignment, the documents, hearings and travel, a pending list, notes, your time, and your expenses. From those it prepares the statement of time and expenses for each case, addressed to the judge who assigned you, and tracks it from submitted to paid. It is built for Texas judges sitting by assignment and runs on iPhone and iPad.

What should I set up first?

The first screens ask who you are, how you are paid, where your mileage starts, and who usually assigns you. Then add your first assignment: the cause, the court, the county seat, and the order of assignment, including the kind of assignment. That one choice sets how your time is counted and which statutes your statements cite. You can start with an invented sample case to look around, and remove it in Settings when you’re ready.

Why must I use Face ID or a passcode?

Because the app holds court records. It locks every time it opens and again after a minute in the background. The check is done by iOS; the app never sees your face data or passcode.

Assignments and pay

Which kinds of assignment does it handle?

Five, each with its own statute: a regional assignment by one of the eleven presiding judges of the administrative judicial regions (Tex. Gov’t Code § 74.056); a statutory probate court assignment by the presiding judge of the statutory probate courts (§ 25.0022(h)); a county court probate, guardianship, or mental health matter (§§ 26.012, 25.0022(h)); a visiting judge appointed by the county judge (§§ 26.023, 26.026); and a temporary justice of the peace (§ 27.055(b)). For anything else, choose Other and type the authority your order cites.

Why is my time in days on one case and hours on another?

Because the statutes differ. Under Chapter 74, sitting more than half a day is a full day and half a day or less is a half day (§ 74.061(j)); the app counts each date once. Section 25.0022 has no half-day rule, and the statutory probate courts’ report form asks for actual hours, in person and remote, so a statutory probate case is always in hours: a 15-minute review of a motion is 0.25 hour, not a half day.

What does a statutory probate statement look like?

It follows the Assigned Statutory Probate Judge’s Report: the assignment, total hours worked in person and remotely, the days with in-person service, the expense lines, and the form’s certification, followed by a sheet of hours and dates and the itemized expenses with receipts. It claims no dollar amount for time, because the presiding judge determines the salary.

How is mileage figured?

Round trip from the origin you set, at the rate in effect on the date of travel: $0.725 a mile from January 1, 2026, the rate the Office of Court Administration uses. Past years’ rates are built in, and you can change a rate in Settings if your assigning judge uses another. Lodging, meals, and other expenses are claimed at actual cost, with receipts attached to the statement.

What about the $25 per diem, or a statutory county court?

Log the per diem as its own expense; a past in-person hearing offers Log per diem once you set your per diem in Settings. Chapter 74 pays it for each day or fraction of a day outside your district or county (§ 74.061(f)); § 25.0022(p) provides the same for statutory probate assignments. For an assignment to a statutory county court, turn off Time is compensated for this assignment on the case: the State pays no compensation for that sitting (§ 74.061(k)), so your time is listed for the record while your expenses are still claimed.

Where do the explanations and citations come from?

Tap any ⓘ for the rule in plain words and the statute behind it. They summarize the Government Code as current on January 1, 2026, and the Office of Court Administration’s travel guidance. They are not legal advice, and your assigning judge’s own procedures govern the paperwork.

Statements

Who receives my statement?

The judge who assigned the case, chosen on each case: any of the eleven regional presiding judges, the presiding judge of the statutory probate courts, or someone you type in, such as a county judge. Names and addresses come from the Office of Court Administration’s published list and can be corrected on the case if an office changes.

How do I send one, and how do I keep track?

Open Prepare statement on the case. Review it, then print or save the PDF, or email it with the receipts attached. Mark it submitted when you send it: its entries lock, so nothing is ever claimed twice, and the next statement starts fresh. Mark it forwarded for payment and paid as it moves along; Time & Statements shows where every statement stands.

Your records

Where is my data? Is there an account?

No account and no server of ours. Everything lives on your device and nothing reaches us. See the privacy policy, which is short because there is little to disclose.

How do I back up, or move to a new iPad?

In Settings, Back up with documents writes everything to one file that you save where you like: Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or email. On the new device, choose Restore from a backup when the app first opens, or Restore in Settings later. Restoring merges with what is already there, so you can also use a backup to bring an iPhone and an iPad into agreement. A device backup or Apple’s Quick Start also carries the app’s data to a new device.

Can I use larger text?

Yes. The app follows the text size set in Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size. At larger sizes, the iPad switches to the single-column layout so nothing is squeezed.

Subscription

What does it cost?

The download is free. A subscription is $14.99 for the first year, then $49.99 a year. It renews automatically unless you turn it off at least 24 hours before the renewal date. Payment is charged to your Apple Account.

Why a subscription?

Because the rules change. The Legislature amends the statutes, the mileage rate changes each January, and presiding judges and their offices change. The subscription pays for keeping the app’s rules, rates, directory, and explanations current.

How do I cancel, or restore a purchase on a new device?

Cancel any time in Settings → your name → Subscriptions on your iPhone or iPad. To use your subscription on another device signed into the same Apple Account, tap Restore purchases in the app’s Settings.

What happens if my subscription ends?

Your records stay yours. The app becomes read-only: you can open every case, print past statements, and back up or export everything, but you can’t add or change anything until you renew.
