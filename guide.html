1. Setting up

Install and unlock

Install Visiting Judges Case Manager from the App Store. Its home-screen label is Case Manager. The app asks for Face ID (or your passcode) every time it opens and again after a minute in the background, because it holds court records. If your device has no passcode, set one in the device’s Settings first.

The first screens

  1. Who you are: your name and title as they should read on your statements, your mailing address, email, and phone.
  2. How you are paid: by the day (most retired, senior, and former judges), county reimbursed, or expenses only; your full-day rate if you want statements to show the amount; the state per diem if you claim it; and where your mileage is measured from.
  3. Who usually assigns you: one of the eleven regional presiding judges, the presiding judge of the statutory probate courts, or someone you type in. Each case can name a different one.
  4. Start with the sample or start empty. The sample is an invented case that shows every part of the app; remove it in Settings whenever you like. Moving from another device? Choose Restore from a backup… on the first screen instead (see Backups).

Subscribe

Adding or changing records requires the subscription: $14.99 the first year, then $49.99 a year, billed by Apple. The sample case stays open to edit without one. If a subscription lapses, the app becomes read-only: every case opens, statements print, and backups work, but nothing can be added or changed until you renew. Settings → Subscription has Restore purchases and Manage subscription.

3. Your first assignment

Tap New assignment (Dashboard) or Cases → New, and enter the cause number, style, court, county, county seat, courtroom address, and the one-way miles from your starting point. Then the order of assignment:

  • Kind of assignment — regional (Chapter 74), statutory probate, a county court probate or mental-health matter, appointed by the county judge, temporary justice of the peace, or other. This one choice sets how your time counts (full and half days, or actual hours) and which statutes your statements cite.
  • Assigned by — the judge who assigned this case; its statements go there.
  • Type of case — civil, criminal, decedent’s estate, guardianship, or trust. It decides which deadlines and bonds are listed first.
  • Scope — all matters, or limited (flagged wherever the case appears).

Add the signed order under Documents → Add documents, and counsel and the clerk under Overview → Add contact, so Email everyone reaches them.

4. Documents, time, and expenses

  • Documents: Add documents from Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or anywhere on the device, or Photograph a document. Each is filed as “YYYY.MM.DD - Title”. Tap one to read it; Details changes its title, type, or status.
  • Time: Log time. A Chapter 74 case counts a full day or a half day (each date once); a statutory probate case counts actual hours, in person or remote.
  • Expenses: Mileage trip figures the round trip at the rate in effect on that date; Add expense handles meals, lodging, and the rest; Take photo attaches the receipt, which becomes an exhibit on the statement.

5. Court settings and trips

Add court setting on the case’s Court settings tab (or the Dashboard or Calendar). Choose the kind (hearing, trial, pretrial or status conference, mediation, or other), the first and last day if it runs several days (court days only, unless you turn that off), the time, and in person or remote. Add to calendar puts each day you sit on the calendar you chose in Settings.

After you save an in-person setting, the app offers Plan travel, with dates suggested by the distance: leave the day before at 150 miles or more, and come home the day after at 250 or more. A trip can serve one setting, several, or none. Once it begins, Log trip expenses records the round-trip mileage and the per diem for each day away, then opens an expense for the hotel bill.

To change or delete a setting or a trip, tap Edit on it.

6. Deadlines and reminders

  1. On the case’s Deadlines tab, tap Start deadlines….
  2. Pick what happened — letters granted, a guardian appointed, a judgment signed, a Rule 91a or summary-judgment motion filed, sentence imposed, a habeas application received, a TRO signed, and so on — and the date it happened.
  3. The app proposes each deadline that runs from it, with its citation and the due date counted the rule’s way. Uncheck any that don’t apply, and tap Add.

A period that ends on a weekend or national holiday runs to the next court day, and a period of five days or less skips them throughout. A deadline you owe is never moved later. When a fiduciary qualifies, mark Qualify met and the deadlines that run from qualification are offered; mark a yearly account met and next year’s is added.

Motions to extend: open the deadline, tap Motion to extend filed… (a to-do to rule on it is added), then Granted… with the new date and signed order, or Denied. Extend by order… is for the court’s own motion.

Reminders arrive at 8:00 a.m., 30 days, 7 days, and 1 day before each deadline and on the day. Turn them on in Settings → Deadline reminders. Unless you choose to show case names, the lock screen says only that a deadline is due. Open deadlines are also copied to your calendar.

7. Bonds

Every kind of bond is available in every case; the case’s type only decides which are listed first. On the case’s Bonds tab, tap Add bond and choose the kind: fiduciaries (executor, administrator, temporary, guardian, trustee), injunctions, supersedeas, receiverships, attachment, garnishment, sequestration and replevy, security for costs and statements of inability, appeals from justice court, or bail. Each keeps its amount, form, sureties, dates, and a history of every change by order.

  • Enter figures on an executor’s, administrator’s, guardian’s, or management trustee’s bond after each inventory, account, or sale. The app compares the bond with the statutory formula (personal property plus 12 months’ revenue, less deposits) and flags a bond that may be insufficient or may be reducible. Set the margin in Settings → Bonds. The decision is always yours.
  • Change amount records an increase, a reduction, or a new bond by order.
  • For supersedeas, enter the damages, interest, costs, and net worth, and the app figures the security under Rule 24.2.
  • Bond deadlines… opens the bond events in Start deadlines.

8. Statements

Tap Prepare statement on a case. Review it, then print, save a PDF, or email it to the assigning judge with the receipts attached (a Mail draft you send yourself). When it is sent, tap Mark submitted…: its entries lock, so nothing is claimed twice, and the next statement starts fresh. Later, Mark forwarded to county… and Mark paid…. A statutory probate case prints on the presiding judge’s report form, in hours.

9. Backups and new devices

Settings → Backup & other devices → Back up with documents writes everything to one file that you save where you like: Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or email. On a new device, choose Restore from a backup… on the first screen, or Settings → Restore… later. Restoring merges: for each record the newer copy wins, so restoring the same file twice never duplicates anything, and a backup can bring an iPhone and an iPad into agreement.

Your records live only on your device. Back up regularly, and keep the backup somewhere you control.
