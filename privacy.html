Privacy Policy

Visiting Judges Case Manager for Windows · Effective November 21, 2026

The short version: the program sends nothing to us, ever. Your cases, documents, time, expenses, and statements stay on your PC. They leave it only when you send, save, or back them up yourself.

What the program stores, and where

Everything you put into Visiting Judges Case Manager is stored as files in your own Windows account’s local application-data folder on that PC (Settings → Privacy → Show where my records are kept opens it): your profile, each assignment and its order, the documents you add, court settings and trips, deadlines, bonds, pending items, notes, time and expense entries, and your statements and their status. That includes information about other people that a case naturally carries, such as the names of parties and the names and email addresses of counsel and clerks. The folder is a local one, not a roaming profile that a network might copy to its servers. There is no server of ours and no account system, and the program does not send your records anywhere on its own.

Uninstalling the program leaves your records in that folder, so that reinstalling finds them. If your PC or your county backs up that folder, the backup includes your records like any other files, under that backup’s own terms; we never see it.

The lock

The program asks for Windows Hello, or for a PIN you choose for it, each time it opens, after a minute away, and whenever Windows locks or sleeps. Windows Hello is performed by Windows: the program learns only whether you unlocked it. A PIN is stored only as a one-way hash, which cannot be turned back into the PIN. The lock keeps the program closed; the record files themselves are protected by your Windows sign-in and, where your PC uses it, BitLocker or other drive encryption.

When your information leaves the PC

Only when you tell it to, and only to the place you choose:

  • Email. When you email a statement, the program saves the statement and its receipts to a folder and opens a draft in your own email program; you attach the files, review the message, and send it through your own email account.
  • Saving and printing. A statement you save as a PDF or print goes where you send it.
  • Your calendar. Save calendar file writes your upcoming court settings, trips, and open deadlines to a file where you choose. If you open it in Outlook or import it into Google Calendar, that service keeps those events under its own terms.
  • Backups. A backup is one file that you save wherever you choose, such as a folder on the PC, a USB drive, or OneDrive. It stays under your control.

Reminders

Deadline reminders are Windows notifications that the program shows itself, on the PC. No push service or server of ours is involved, and nothing about a reminder leaves the PC. Unless you choose to show case names, a reminder says only that a deadline is due.

Updates: the one network request

The program asks GitHub, where new versions are published, whether a newer version exists, and downloads it if so: about a minute after it starts, then every six hours. The request carries none of your records or personal information; like any download, it reveals your computer’s internet address to GitHub, which handles it under its own privacy terms. You can turn automatic checks off in Settings → Updates.

Your code

The personal code that lets you add and change records names you and the date it is good through. The program checks it on the PC; nothing about it, or about your use of the program, is reported anywhere. The publisher keeps a list of the codes issued (the name, email address, and date for each), and uses it only to renew or withdraw codes.

What the program does not do

The program contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking of any kind, and no third-party data-collection software. It does not read your contacts, your location, your camera, or your other files: it opens only the files you choose in its own Open and Save windows.

Court records are yours to protect

The program keeps your records on your PC precisely because they are court records. You remain responsible for handling them as the law and your court’s rules require: for example, keeping the PC locked, deciding what to back up and where, and taking care with what you email or share.

Children

The program is made for judges and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

If a future version ever changes any of the above, for example by adding sync between your computers, this policy will be updated first, with its effective date revised and the change described plainly.
